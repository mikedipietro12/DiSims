Three directions

Same scenes in each: a saturated PbI₂ beaker with its zoomed particle window, the ion set, the Q vs Ksp gauge in its three states, and the real precipitate colours. Precipitates keep their real colours in every direction. Every ion carries its symbol and charge, so none of them relies on colour alone.

Round 2: solid colours, acids and bases

Direction A is locked. Two follow-ups. First, ions in a solid now take the crystal's real colour and switch to rose or amber once dissolved; for PbI₂ the amber iodide barely changes, so a slightly more orange amber is shown beside it. Second, acids and bases need their own colours site-wide: H⁺ (and H₃O⁺) and OH⁻ never use the plain cation or anion colours.
