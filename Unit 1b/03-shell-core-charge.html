      How does the data support our understanding of the organization of the electrons in this atom?

      The electrons in the outermost shell of an atom are called valence electrons. Electrons in shells closer to the nucleus are inner-shell electrons. Lithium has one valence electron and two inner-shell electrons. Hydrogen has one valence electron and no inner-shell electrons.

      The nucleus plus the inner shells of electrons make up the core of the atom, and the net overall charge on the core is the core charge. Configuration B shows lithium written that way: a core of charge +1 with one valence electron outside it.

      Core charge
      Core charge = Nucleus charge (Z) − Inner shell electrons  →  3 − 2 = +1

      The valence electron feels the core charge, not the full nuclear charge. That is why lithium’s ionization energy is only 0.52 MJ/mol.
