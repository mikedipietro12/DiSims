      As we’ve seen in this simulation, as the “Q” value of the nucleus increases, the “pull” on the electron also increases, meaning the energy required to remove that electron also increases. The required energy to remove an electron to an “infinite distance away” is called ionization energy. Why do you think it’s called that?

      The experimental value for the ionization energy of lithium is actually 0.52 MJ/mol. What does that tell us about the organization of the electrons in lithium?

          Practice

          Write all of the IE equations for the particles on this line. Do not solve the equations — just think about the values of q1, q2, and d.

          1. Write the three Coulombic Potential Energy terms for the helium atom model in Figure 2. Assume that the distance between each electron and the nucleus is d and that the distance between the two electrons is 2d.
          2. Based on your answer to part a) explain why the IE of He is slightly less than twice the IE of H even though both atoms are about the same size.
          Model answers
          1. Nucleus–electron 1: V = k(+2)(−1)/d = −2k/d
            Nucleus–electron 2: V = k(+2)(−1)/d = −2k/d
            Electron–electron: V = k(−1)(−1)/(2d) = +k/(2d)
          2. The electron–electron term is positive (repulsive), so it raises the total potential energy and makes each electron a little easier to remove. Without that repulsion you would expect IE(He) ≈ 2 × IE(H) at the same size; the repulsion is why helium’s IE is slightly less than twice hydrogen’s.
