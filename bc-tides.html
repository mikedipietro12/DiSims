How this works. Each beach has its own harmonic tide model — 8 astronomical constituents plus a few shallow-water overtones — fit to published predictions, with K2 and P1 inferred from S2 and K1. The Moon's position, phase and distance come from a low-precision lunar ephemeris. The Moon–Earth panel is the equilibrium picture: the bulges point at the Moon, which is the tidal forcing. The real ocean lags — high water here arrives roughly 4½ hours after the Moon is overhead — so the chart peaks well after the beach rotates past the bulge. The dot's glow tracks its actual tide height so you can see that delay.

Accuracy. Jericho Beach uses the Vancouver (Burrard Inlet) station about 7 km away, so expect a few extra minutes of timing offset there. ~8–10 cm typical in the fitting window, drifting to ~15–25 cm and up to an hour of timing error months away from spring 2026. Good for seeing the tide and planning a beach walk — not for navigation. The beach view and tidal-current arrow are schematic: the current direction and strength come from the rate the tide is changing (fastest mid-tide, slack at the turns), not a current meter.
